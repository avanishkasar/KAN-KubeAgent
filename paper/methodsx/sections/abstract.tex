\begin{abstract}
Deciding when to stop a training job is usually left to a person reading loss curves or to a fixed rule whose reasons cannot be inspected. This article describes how to build a stopping gate that is an explicit formula. A Kolmogorov--Arnold Network (KAN) reads five causal features of a running job, and after training each of its edges is fixed to a closed-form function, so the formula an operator reads is exactly the function that decides. The gate is trained by hindsight supervision: every decision point of a completed run is labelled with the validation-loss improvement that was actually still to come, and one tolerance sets the acceptable loss of quality. On \nRuns{} real training runs from four image-classification workloads, a gate trained on three workloads and applied to the fourth saved \R{lowo}{kan}{saved}\% of training epochs at \R{lowo}{kan}{regret}\% mean regret in best validation loss, against \R{lowo}{kanrule}{saved}\% for the same network trained to imitate a hand-written rule. Its mean regret stayed at or below \X{PT@kanworst}\% on every held-out workload, whereas a patience rule tuned on the other workloads reached \X{PT@1@worst}\% on one. In live control of \X{PL@ks@n} training processes, the gate used as stop rule with a reduce-on-plateau learning-rate schedule reached the same best validation loss as standard practice (the same schedule with patience-based stopping) on every run but used \X{PL@ks@used} instead of \X{PL@plat@used} of 1{,}920 epochs, and the gate's own learning-rate action was no better than the plateau schedule. The method therefore offers an auditable, outcome-trained stop rule rather than larger savings.
\begin{itemize}
\item Hindsight labels, the improvement each decision point actually had ahead of it, replace imitation of hand-written rules as the training signal for a stopping gate.
\item Symbolic fixing of the KAN's edges makes the extracted formula the decision function itself; agreement is checked numerically for every gate (largest difference \X{faithMax} of 100 score points).
\item Validation uses real runs only, two cross-validation protocols, a patience rule tuned under the same protocol, and live comparison with standard practice, and reports where the method does not win.
\end{itemize}
\end{abstract}
